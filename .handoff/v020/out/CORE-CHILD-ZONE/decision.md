| D404 | Primary parent and child zones on one node | (1) Cause of the permanent SERVFAIL (`zone awaiting committed publication`) of a primary child zone under a primary parent: a cycle of two exact checks. Every committed-status write advances a `DNSZone`'s resourceVersion, so a lifecycle receipt is often behind its `DNSZone` until the driver realigns it. Fencing a child in its parent (`parent-fence`, `late-parent-fence`, `parent-release`) and the child's lifecycle transition that records or releases that obligation required the exact receipt of both zones, in the SQLite adapter (`source`) and again in `currentguard.checkBackground`. Realigning either receipt is itself a lifecycle CAS whose membership check needs exactly that fence. So a parent whose receipt was behind could never fence a new child, and a child whose receipt was behind (a status write before its fence, or under a late parent) could never be fenced; both stayed unavailable until a restart, which re-read the same state and usually got past it before the next status write. Rule: a command that changes only a parent/child routing boundary (the three boundary admissions with a parent, and a lifecycle transition that changes nothing but `Sequence` and `Parents`) needs the same zone (object name, UID, apex, type, and a source that is not removed), not the exact receipt (`boundarySource`, `BackgroundCheck.Boundary`, honored by the guard only for those reasons). It never advances the receipt; every other command and the realignment itself keep the exact check. (2) Serving across the pair's own transitions: a parent with a desired child that is not yet fenced keeps serving its committed head (the child's lifecycle entry or desired-only coverage is the longer routing match at and below its apex) and is not admitted (UPDATE, NOTIFY, status and publication acknowledgment wait), as D395 does for a zone's own write; a child whose obligation is witnessed by a parent that is still Fencing (a late parent) keeps serving and stays admitted, and the parent serves only once Active. Before this the parent answered SERVFAIL from a child's creation until its fence was published (up to 0.8 s) and the child from a late parent's fence until that parent activated (0.5 s). | A deployment with `example.` and `child.example.` as primaries on one node is ordinary, and an authoritative zone that stays SERVFAIL until a restart is a release defect. The boundary commands neither read nor record the zone's configuration (the adapter's `candidate` already allows only the one child reservation to change), so the exact resourceVersion and intent add no safety there and only couple them to the status writer; zone identity is still exact. Answering the parent's last committed head while its child is unfenced is the state the node served just before the child was created, and every name the child owns already routes to the child. | Making the driver realign receipts before fencing (rejected: the realignment CAS needs the fence, which is the cycle, and a status write can land between the two steps); suppressing status writes while a fence is pending (rejected: couples the status writer to driver internals and leaves the late-parent and recreate cases); relaxing the exact receipt for all lifecycle transitions (rejected: phase changes and source realignment must observe the exact source); keeping the parent unavailable until the child's fence is published (rejected: a SERVFAIL for every name of the parent whenever a child zone is created). |
