| D369 | Managed root anchors under enrolled HA | **Serving and supervision (F28-HA-1 and F28-HA-5).** Root admission for managed anchors is split. Serving admission accepts every role that can hold this member's accepted owner: writable, follower, fenced and conflict. A fenced or conflicted cut is prepared, checked and published only with its token's own DNS-only disposition (D360), in direct and edges topology and across a physical restart; edges still require the complete fleet observation under the original token. Updater admission is unchanged: only the elected writer of the original term polls as a writer. Every role without an updater keeps the deny-only supervisor (D318), one child per member, term and role; a role change inside a term joins the old child and admits one new child, and ordinary installs still cannot rearm a terminal result. The poller and the updater wait when the local wall clock trails the committed checkpoint by at most `trustanchors.ClockTolerance` (five minutes) and stay terminal beyond it; the wait acquires nothing and creates no sample or state. A poller child that has started no acquisition and whose last operation only deferred for its clock was neither held nor refused (D318): its next fresh capture may adopt a newer installed incarnation in the same member, term and role, as the supervisor's readiness wait does. A refused or failed acquisition still cannot move, and nothing crosses a role change. **History (F28-HA-2).** Two writers of one history count their own owner generations and stamp their own clocks, so a different managed owner is ordered by authority term before generation. `InstallReplica` adopts it when the image's term has a strictly higher epoch than the installed image's term, and `ResolveConflict`/`ResolveAudited` adopt the branch they keep in the transaction that allocates the new term, whatever its envelope generation, pure generation or `UpdatedAt`. Adoption still requires the same ClusterID, HistoryID, InitializedAt, PolicyID, SeedDigest and DomainID and `RetainsRecoveryHistory`. A managed owner is never replaced by a dormant one; an unchanged owner, the first install and dormant-to-managed keep their rules; every install inside one term keeps the strict successor rule. Adoption also requires the adopted owner to revoke every key the abandoned owner revoked and every key in this member's node-local denial, the rule an install inside one term already applies to a pending denial; otherwise the install or resolution is refused with `accepted.ErrCAS` before any write. The refused member stays fenced and keeps answering DNS-only from its own branch, which already revoked the key (or, after a local denial, stays retired as before), takes no later image of the new term, and adopts on the first scheduled pull after the canonical writer records the same tombstone. If that writer never can, the member never follows it again (see "What ends a refused state"). The node-local carrier is rebased in the same transaction as the owner rows, the image and the quarantine of the abandoned branch: every carried key stays in `Denied` (a receipt that names a generation above the adopted owner is restated at it), and `RetiredThrough` becomes the adopted generation minus one; a terminal or saturated carrier stays terminal. The retirement gate takes that exact floor through `Change.StageRebase`, the only transition that can lower it: it needs an installed mark of the same domain and a floor at or above the adopted generation minus one, clears the registration, and ends every write attempt captured before it, because the adopted owner can be one the member held earlier. Other members still adopt a resolved branch only through scheduled pulls (D361). **What ends a refused state (F28-HA-2 recheck).** A refusal changes nothing on the member and leaves anchor admission open; the member repeats the pull every interval. No recovery clears a carrier, installs a branch that lacks a carried key, or replaces managed history with a dormant or static owner. A carried revocation ends at the canonical writer's next refresh, and the member must see every carried key revoked. It never ends when that writer can never record the key: the root has stopped publishing it (the writer keeps it Missing, which stays trusted), or the canonical history never held it (a REVOKE of an unknown key is not recorded). Managed history against a dormant primary ends when that primary bootstraps managed anchors from the same seeds: its first managed owner is another branch of the same history and resolver domain and is adopted by term. A recovery-history disagreement (a second recovery writes another record), other seeds, a revocation the writer can never record and a terminal carrier end only by replacing the member with a newly joined receiver (D355), which takes the primary's owner and a carrier that denies exactly the keys that owner revokes. The replacement needs its own node name and internal address, because enrollment refuses both for an active member and no member revocation is mounted; the replaced member stays in the roster. A member whose own check denied a key stays retired after its promotion until its own observation of the REVOKE commits the tombstone. A REVOKE verified while an install replaces the owner it was captured for closes anchor admission for the process (D297); a restart binds the committed cut and the next check records it. A refusing member cannot be promoted (it is not a follower), so every other recovery needs the current primary or a member that follows it. **Abandoning a lost term (second recheck).** When that primary and every member that followed it are lost for good, `cluster resolution --offline` reviews a fenced member that holds no image of its current term and no conflict or divergence as `fenced_behind_term` (resolvable), and `cluster resolve --offline --abandon-term <epoch>` keeps that member's own installed branch under a new term in the same audited resolution transaction (D361). The store takes the exact reviewed term as `ResolveRequest.AbandonTerm` and requires `Fenced` mode, no conflict or divergence, that exact term, an installed image of the same cluster at a lower epoch and the member's own installed image as the kept branch; otherwise `replication.ErrCAS` with nothing written. Without `AbandonTerm` the store refuses such a member as before, and the CLI refuses a `fenced_behind_term` review without the exact epoch and `--abandon-term` on any other review. The kept owner and node-local carrier are unchanged, so every revocation and pending denial carries into the new term (a pending denial still ends only with that writer's own observation of the REVOKE); the audit authorization names the abandoned term; a returning member of the abandoned term is fenced by the new term and adopts the kept branch under the rules above.
