| D369 | Managed root anchors under enrolled HA | **Serving and supervision (F28-HA-1 and F28-HA-5).** Root admission for managed anchors is split. Serving admission accepts every role that can hold this member's accepted owner: writable, follower, fenced and conflict. A fenced or conflicted cut is prepared, checked and published only with its token's own DNS-only disposition (D360), in direct and edges topology and across a physical restart; edges still require the complete fleet observation under the original token. Updater admission is unchanged: only the elected writer of the original term polls as a writer. Every role without an updater keeps the deny-only supervisor (D318), one child per member, term and role; a role change inside a term joins the old child and admits one new child, and ordinary installs still cannot rearm a terminal result. The poller and the updater wait when the local wall clock trails the committed checkpoint by at most `trustanchors.ClockTolerance` (five minutes) and stay terminal beyond it; the wait acquires nothing and creates no sample or state. A poller child that has started no acquisition and whose last operation only deferred for its clock was neither held nor refused (D318): its next fresh capture may adopt a newer installed incarnation in the same member, term and role, as the supervisor's readiness wait does. A refused or failed acquisition still cannot move, and nothing crosses a role change. **History (F28-HA-2).** Two writers of one history count their own owner generations and stamp their own clocks, so a different managed owner is ordered by authority term before generation. `InstallReplica` adopts it when the image's term has a strictly higher epoch than the installed image's term, and `ResolveConflict`/`ResolveAudited` adopt the branch they keep in the transaction that allocates the new term, whatever its envelope generation, pure generation or `UpdatedAt`. Adoption still requires the same ClusterID, HistoryID, InitializedAt, PolicyID, SeedDigest and DomainID and `RetainsRecoveryHistory`. A managed owner is never replaced by a dormant one; an unchanged owner, the first install and dormant-to-managed keep their rules; every install inside one term keeps the strict successor rule. Adoption also requires the adopted owner to revoke every key the abandoned owner revoked and every key in this member's node-local denial, the rule an install inside one term already applies to a pending denial; otherwise the install or resolution is refused with `accepted.ErrCAS` before any write. The refused member stays fenced and keeps answering DNS-only from its own branch, which already revoked the key (or, after a local denial, stays retired as before), takes no later image of the new term, and adopts on the first scheduled pull after the canonical writer records the same tombstone. If that writer never can, the member never follows it again: no command clears a carried denial, and the operator replaces the member with a newly joined receiver or keeps it out of service. The node-local carrier is rebased in the same transaction as the owner rows, the image and the quarantine of the abandoned branch: every carried key stays in `Denied` (a receipt that names a generation above the adopted owner is restated at it), and `RetiredThrough` becomes the adopted generation minus one; a terminal or saturated carrier stays terminal. The retirement gate takes that exact floor through `Change.StageRebase`, the only transition that can lower it: it needs an installed mark of the same domain and a floor at or above the adopted generation minus one, clears the registration, and ends every write attempt captured before it, because the adopted owner can be one the member held earlier. Other members still adopt a resolved branch only through scheduled pulls (D361). | Root admission accepted only the writable and follower roles, so a managed node answered SERVFAIL for every query, authoritative data included, from the moment it was fenced or conflicted, and refused to start again; every follower passes through the fenced role at each promotion. A member can stay DNS-only for days, and a revoked root key must not stay trusted there. A terminal `ErrClock` after a few seconds of member clock skew ended revocation polling, or the updater, for a whole term with one log line. Strict generation order cannot hold across divergent writer branches, because each side increments its own generation: an old primary one observation ahead of its promoted follower, or the losing side of a conflict, could not install the authoritative branch until that branch's generation passed its own (about a day at the real root, never while that updater is terminal), and `UpdatedAt` compared two members' clocks.
