| D369 | Managed root anchors under enrolled HA | **Serving and supervision (F28-HA-1 and F28-HA-5).** Root admission for managed anchors is split. Serving admission accepts every role that can hold this member's accepted owner: writable, follower, fenced and conflict. A fenced or conflicted cut is prepared, checked and published only with its token's own DNS-only disposition (D360), in direct and edges topology and across a physical restart; edges still require the complete fleet observation under the original token. Updater admission is unchanged: only the elected writer of the original term polls as a writer. Every role without an updater keeps the deny-only supervisor (D318), one child per member, term and role; a role change inside a term joins the old child and admits one new child, and ordinary installs still cannot rearm a terminal result. The poller and the updater wait when the local wall clock trails the committed checkpoint by at most `trustanchors.ClockTolerance` (five minutes) and stay terminal beyond it; the wait acquires nothing and creates no sample or state. A poller child that has started no acquisition and whose last operation only deferred for its clock was neither held nor refused (D318): its next fresh capture may adopt a newer installed incarnation in the same member, term and role, as the supervisor's readiness wait does. A refused or failed acquisition still cannot move, and nothing crosses a role change. **History (F28-HA-2; decided here, implemented by package F28-HISTORY and not by the F28-FENCED commits).** An install performed under a higher authority term, or an audited `cluster resolve`, adopts the authoritative branch's managed owner even when its generation is not greater than the local one, provided ClusterID, HistoryID, InitializedAt, PolicyID, SeedDigest and DomainID match and the recovery history is retained. A key the local branch revoked that the adopted owner still trusts stays denied in the node-local carrier until the canonical branch revokes it. Same-term installs keep the strict successor rule. | Root admission accepted only the writable and follower roles, so a managed node answered SERVFAIL for every query, authoritative data included, from the moment it was fenced or conflicted, and refused to start again; every follower passes through the fenced role at each promotion. A member can stay DNS-only for days, and a revoked root key must not stay trusted there. A terminal `ErrClock` after a few seconds of member clock skew ended revocation polling, or the updater, for a whole term with one log line. Strict generation order cannot hold across divergent writer branches, because each side increments its own generation. | Serving a fenced managed node from static or shipped anchors; reopening HTTP, authentication or writer work on a DNS-only cut; letting a fenced or conflicted member advance or bootstrap trust; no supervision while fenced or conflicted (a revoked key stays trusted until rejoin); carrying a terminal poll result across a role change; a settle delay before the first DNS-only poll; applying the tolerance by fabricating samples or crediting hold-down; an unbounded wait for a checkpoint far ahead; pinning a waiting poller to the incarnation of its first capture (any ordinary install during the wait, which can last five minutes, would end supervision for the term); letting a clock wait move a poller whose acquisition already failed; waiting for the authoritative branch's generation to pass the local one (stalls rejoin for a refresh interval, or forever); dropping a local revocation the adopted branch has not seen |
