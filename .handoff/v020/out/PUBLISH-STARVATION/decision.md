| D405 | Publication under steady commits | Two causes of a node falling behind its own commits are removed. (1) A SQLite commit that moves a `ZoneState` head to new content deletes, in its own transaction, every `ZoneChunk` that no head references any more: it marks the root manifest and parts of every head, tombstoned ones included (`zonestate.References`), and deletes the rest, which also removes chunks an earlier version left behind; when a head's root cannot be read nothing is deleted, a warning names the head, and the commit still lands. Because a repository read takes the head and its chunks in separate store reads, a missing chunk is checked against the head again: a moved head is read again (at most 16 times, then `ErrConflict`), a head that did not move answers `ErrInvalid`, so `store.ErrNotFound` from `Repository.Read` keeps meaning only "no head", which the lifecycle callers rely on. Before, every content commit (each signed UPDATE, each applied record-set change) left the whole previous zone content in the replicated image, and every publication reads, digests, validates and decodes the whole image several times, so the time to publish grew with every UPDATE (230 chunks, 3.8 MB, after 220 commits of a 20-name zone; a preparation from 0.1 s to more than 2 s without the race detector). (2) `Publisher.Wake` no longer cancels the preparation in progress. The Wake count is noted before a preparation reads its candidate; Wakes that arrive after that are answered by exactly one more preparation once it has published. A new generation, authority or dependency cursor still cancels, because the obsolete cut's publication would be refused. Also: `Retry-After: 1` is no longer sent on a refusal after an earlier command or item of the same request dispatched (`servinghttp.Dispatched`), and the docs say what an answer without it means. An object write answered `503 unavailable` ("current writable serving generation unavailable") is effect-free in every case (no step after an object command's commit can refuse), so test helpers no longer treat it as an unknown outcome. Exact currency is unchanged everywhere: HTTP capture, command admission, the seal of a sign-in, UPDATE acknowledgment (D313), fences, revocations and the D378 chain. | Measured, not assumed: an instrumented publisher showed preparation time rising linearly with the revision and a CPU profile put the time in whole-image `replication.Validate`; a per-kind dump of the image found the chunks; 67 of 144 cancellations in a 30 + 30 run were Wakes at about 15 µs into a preparation for the same commit. With both changes the mounted load test without the race detector admits 200 of 200 sign-ins, record-set writes and reads, each sent once, under ten signed UPDATEs a second (in five of six runs: `diag08`, `diag09`, `norace15`, the reviewer's `review-04` and `fix-08`; the reviewer's `review-03` at load 18 to 20 refused 12 reads at capture; before: 225 to 254 of 400 refused at capture in `load05`/`load06`), at under one committed UPDATE a second (most UPDATEs answer SERVFAIL, D399), and each write is answered in DNS within 2.8 s of its 200 in `fix-08`. | Letting a preparation finish and publish a not-newest generation (HTTP capture, command admission and acknowledgment need the newest one, so such a publication helps DNS only, and it delays the current one by a whole preparation); capturing reads and sign-ins on the newest complete publication instead of refusing them (rejected in D378: a revoked credential or a just-acknowledged write would be read from older data); raising the two-second capture wait (rejected in D378; under the race detector a publication still outlasts it); memoizing whole-image validation across owners (D378: their callbacks must be proven pure first; it is the largest remaining share and the next step); incremental per-zone preparation (a redesign of the preparation pipeline); a chunk sweep outside the commit (a second writer and a window with unreachable chunks); a Kubernetes chunk collector in this package (its staging is not transactional) |
