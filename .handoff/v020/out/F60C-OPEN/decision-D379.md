| D379 | Tier-B hook event production, the delivery lane and negotiated roles | Root produces hook events on each hook instance's elected lease from the store's resumable change feed (DNSZone, the private ZoneState head that holds a zone's committed serial, DNSRecordSet, Integration, Kudurru) and the current writer term, read after a durable per-instance `IntegrationEventCursor` (one private object per instance, opaque positions, exact-version CAS). Every 2 s tick (the debounce) queues at most 32 events and nothing while 256 rows are undelivered or 384 exist in all; a kind with no change keeps its position until the store head is 1,024 revisions ahead, or a quarter of the store's feed retention if that is less (so a quiet kind is not compacted past while the ticks keep up), so an idle tick writes nothing (each cursor write itself advances the head); a new instance takes a baseline and reports only later changes; a cursor the feed cannot resume (the store reports it compacted past the cursor, lineage change, too new) yields one `zone.changed` per zone (`change: "resync"`, carrying the committed serial and versioned by that resync, a digest of the positions that could not resume, so each resync reaches the peer once) and a fresh baseline. The delivery ID is SHA-256(instance UID, topic, subject UID, version), and an existing row is skipped, so a crash between queue and cursor commit, or a status write that does not change what a topic reports, queues nothing while that row exists (after it is evicted or pruned, such a status write resends the same ID, which the peer deduplicates; count eviction takes superseded rows, older versions of a subject with a newer row, first, because a status write reproduces only a subject's current version). Topics shipped in v0.2: `record.changed` (generation or deletion), `zone.changed` (committed serial or deletion, from one source per subject: the ZoneState head, or a serial the DNSZone's own status carries, which is the same version; a DNSZone write without a serial is no event), `blocking.toggled` (committed changes only; a timed disable running out writes nothing and is no event), `integration.error` (Ready turned False, versioned by reason and transition time) and `cluster.leader_changed`; the provider advertises exactly these. Deferred: `query.blocked`, `vip.master_changed`, `client.seen`, `client.left`. Guarantees: at-least-once, unordered across retries, versioned payloads, 24 h dead letter; a 4xx answer dead-letters and sets Degraded `EventDeadLettered` (published); a 5xx, an attempt timeout while the caller lives, or an exhausted budget retries (budget after 5 min); a fence, stop or caller cancellation leaves the row untouched. Retention, all by exact UID/RV: each drain evicts the instance's terminal rows above 128 (superseded first, then oldest), so an instance holds at most 384 rows even when its peer acknowledges promptly (under 50,000 for 128 instances); every row of another instance, delivered or not and whether or not its owner exists, is pruned an hour after its deadline, because an owner that runs any lease normally dead-letters its own rows at the deadline, within that hour (not guaranteed: a hook lease whose drain keeps failing before it delivers, or an owner with no lease in that hour, leaves a row another lease deletes without `EventDeadLettered`); hook leases prune on each drain, leases without the hook role every 300 poll intervals (10 minutes at the default 2 s) but at least every quarter of the one-hour retention (15 minutes) whatever the poll interval, when they also dead-letter their own undelivered rows past the deadline without an attempt (a peer that stopped advertising hooks); cursors of vanished instances are pruned; with no lease running at all nothing is pruned until one runs; the retention scan pages through every row and keeps only its own rows and 64 prunable ones, so no listing size stops it. Hook events are cluster-wide: every hook instance sees every namespace, so creating a hooks Integration is a cluster-level privilege until a subscription filter exists. Signing: HMAC-SHA256 of the exact body `{"topic","time","payload"}` (JSON tags frozen for `Event`, `Client`, `Network`) with the separate `hmacKey` (32 to 4096 bytes, same bound as conformance). Budget: one charge per HTTP attempt against the instance's own account (its UID). The webhook provider reports negotiated roles (`host.RoleProvider`) so the host polls only inventory peers and never treats it as a publisher. Reviewed sync and act write one `integration.sync`/`integration.act` audit row in their final transaction, as uncertain (`error`) when the effect failed but that transaction still commits. | Hooks were designed for v0.2 (design record lines 996, 1029, 1041) but nothing produced events, the unmounted lane dead-lettered on a slow answer or a busy budget and never pruned, and one Go type could not be inventory-only. The change feed is the only durable, resumable source present on the writer; a durable cursor makes restart and failover lose nothing without a baseline storm. Deterministic IDs plus a pre-queue existence check make reproduction idempotent without exactly-once claims. | Delivery rows as the cursor (baseline storm, one row per subject retained); an in-memory diff of captures (loses changes across restart and failover); a transactional outbox in every write path (touches every command, no Kubernetes equivalent); advertising all nine designed topics while producing five; treating 429 as retriable (kept permanent, as the existing test and "4xx drops" specify); a shared `webhook/all` budget account; time-only retention (unbounded rows under a prompt peer, and a store-wide listing cap that locked every instance out); oldest-first eviction alone (shortened deduplication to the newest 128 rows of any subject); keeping a live owner's expired undelivered rows (kept forever once its lease no longer had the hook role); `zone.changed` from both the DNSZone generation and the ZoneState serial (two events for a new zone); a resync versioned by serial (deduplicated against the serial's earlier event, so the gap was never announced); following the feed bookmark on every tick (rewrites the cursor every 2 s) |
