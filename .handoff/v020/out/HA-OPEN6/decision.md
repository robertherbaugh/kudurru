# HA-OPEN6 decision row

This replaces the D381 row of `out/HA-OPEN5/decision-D381.md` as a whole (which itself replaced the HA-UPDATE-ROUTING row). D375 is unchanged from `out/HA-OPEN5/decision-D375.md`: HA-OPEN6 re-measured the cut at the head with D378 and D399 and the row stands (see the ledger).

| D381 | RFC 2136 UPDATE from enrolled SQLite followers | An enrolled SQLite follower that is not the ready writer selects the zone in its own complete cut (NOTAUTH otherwise), uses the listener's verified TSIG proof and admits the key against its own current keyring (REFUSED for a missing proof or an unknown key). When its source changed under the request before anything was sent (it installed a newer commit, in a burst usually the previous update's own, or replaced a dependency, so the keyring is no longer current or the forward capture refuses the changed source), it waits, inside the forward's four-second bound, for its own current complete publication (`awaitFollowerPublication`), selects the zone and admits the key again there and captures the forward on that source. The same wait follows a source change that the forward finds only when it acquires the store for the exchange, before the exchange with the primary began (`EnrolledForwarder.Forward` joins `clusterreplica.ErrNotSent` to every error returned before the member client is called); the same command ID is then sent once. A command is sent at most once, and a refusal after the exchange may have begun is never followed by a second send. It then sends one version-1 `dns.update` member command: the exact request without its TSIG record, a `TSIGKey`/`tsig` subject naming the key and its secret-derived credential version, the algorithm, transport and signature deadline, bounded by four seconds, the DNS request and the signature. The primary runs the command on its current writer publication (`runMemberUpdate`): it waits for one within the command's bound (`Publisher.WaitTerm`) and captures it, admits the exact key identity (name, algorithm, credential version) in its own current keyring, then runs the native protocol adapter under its elected writer, so zone selection, grants, prerequisites, atomic commit and publication are identical. It runs the request again, after the next publication, only when nothing was decided or committed: when `captureUpdate` rejected it only because its lease was no longer the current writer publication, its keyring stopped being current or the publication did not admit or route the zone at that moment (`forwardedUpdate.stale`, set before admission), or when every compare-and-swap attempt of `Apply` lost against another writer of the zone and nothing committed (`pr.ErrConflict`, seen through `network.WithOutcome`). A retiring or retired zone is refused at once, since no later publication admits it; a cause that does not clear ends at the command's bound with nothing applied. Every other answer is final, a commit whose publication failed included. The command ID is the update's retry identity in the zone's receipt ledger throughout, so a repeated delivery or attempt returns the retained rcode without a second commit. The zone runtime commits in its own transaction, fenced by the writer, the term and the admitted key identity; unlike a generic member command, the sender's current enrollment is rechecked in a transaction only when the acknowledgment is admitted, so a member revoked during those milliseconds can still commit and only its answer is withheld (such a member holds the TSIG secrets and could send a native UPDATE). The rcode is acknowledged inside the member request's command output scope (an empty member command after the zone runtime's own commit). The follower relays that rcode, signed with the request's key, under an acknowledgment bound to the verified key and unchanged empty response rather than its own data generation. A command that was not sent within the bound, was refused by member admission, or whose exchange was lost answers SERVFAIL and is never retried. Fenced, conflicted, standby and static nodes keep their local SERVFAIL. A native UPDATE sent to the primary is unchanged: right after a commit it can still be answered SERVFAIL before anything is applied. | The v0.2 UPDATE row requires primary routing once follower mode exists. Followers sit behind the same VIPs and DHCP configurations as the primary. DNS has no rcode for "outcome unknown", and RFC 2136 section 4.5 tells the requestor to try its next server on SERVFAIL, which is safe for an unsent update and is the native primary's answer for a committed but unpublished one. The receipt ledger already deduplicates by retry identity, and the zone runtime commits in its own fenced transaction, so the member command adds no second dedup row. Every commit is followed within milliseconds by its publication and by the node's own status and lifecycle writes (a new serial, a realigned lifecycle) and their publications, so a command that arrives right after one meets a publication that is being replaced on the follower, on the primary or both. Answering that with SERVFAIL made a DHCP-like burst through a follower fail: up to 6 of 8 updates in the HA-UPDATE-ROUTING verification, and once the first failed every later one in the burst failed too (30 to 40 ms of consecutive failures). The diagnostic runs of this package found four separate causes: the primary's writer check on a replaced lease (the root burst test fails 4 of 4 without the fix, race08), the follower's keyring check after it installed the previous update's commit (e2e01, e2e03), the primary's zone admission while its lifecycle was being realigned (e2e06), and `Apply` running out of compare-and-swap attempts against those writes (e2e08). None of them decided or committed anything, and both nodes decide again on the newer publication, so waiting for it is the HTTP capture wait applied to UPDATE, not a replay. HA-OPEN6 found a fifth in the same class: the forward capture retains only the token and `WithMemberForward` checks it again at acquisition, so a pull between the two made the follower answer an immediate SERVFAIL (90 to 290 ms) for the third or fourth update of a six-update burst under the production bound in 7 of 7 race runs; a second identical send would have committed. The native UPDATE stays outside D399's recapture because a second capture would admit the same wire message on a second keyring; a forwarded one only re-admits the same key identity (name, algorithm, credential version) on the follower and is decided again by the primary, so its wait is the same pre-dispatch rule as D399. | Refusing on followers with REFUSED or NOTAUTH (clients would have to find the SOA MNAME, and REFUSED claims a policy refusal); trusting the follower's grant check; re-verifying the MAC on the primary (adds nothing against an mTLS-authenticated enrolled member, which holds the same keys); REFUSED while the primary is unreachable (stops RFC 2136 clients from trying the primary); retries, local fallback or replay after reconnection; a generic DNS proxy; keeping SERVFAIL for a source that changed under the request and leaving the retry to the client (most of a burst fails, and an RFC 2136 client that tries its next server sends the update to another follower in the same window); retrying a SERVFAIL after the request reached `Apply` (a committed but unpublished update is final; only a lost compare-and-swap with nothing committed is run again); holding the publication across the forward (stops publication and every other writer); changing the native UPDATE path in this package (its window belongs to the DNS path work of D378 and D395); retrying in the e2e helpers instead of fixing the late source change (HA-OPEN6: the follower UPDATEs of the process tests are now sent once); retrying every `ErrCAS` from Forward (one returned after the exchange began may follow a dispatch) |
