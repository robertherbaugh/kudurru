| D408 | Periodic integration syncs paced from their start, and what that exposed | (1) A successful periodic or explicit sync of an integration job schedules the next periodic sync one interval after it began (`nextSync`), or at once when it ran longer than the interval; before, the timer was reset when `execute` returned, so the gap between two observations was the interval plus the whole sync, including the source commit's wait for a complete serving publication. Syncs of one job still never overlap, so a provider sees at most one sync start per interval; failed and fenced syncs keep their delay from the failure (D284), reconcile still syncs at once. (2) A sync ends with the job's own status write, which gives the stored Integration a resource version the frame lacks until the host captures it again, and root fences a sync on the older frame; the next periodic sync therefore waits for a refresh begun after that write (`Host.captures`, `job.captured`, `job.framed`), which the job requests with `Wake`, and at most one interval after the sync ended (the old schedule). (3) UniFi observation admission accepts a target zone whose only change since the observation's cut is its status (`zoneStatusOnly`: same UID, generation, spec, labels, annotations, finalizers, owners and deletion); the zone driver writes that status after every change of the zone's records (D404), and the instance's authority digest already depends only on the zone's UID, activity and spec. Any other change still fences; the target evidence names the zone as read in the commit. (4) On the complete SQLite runtime the integration host starts with the node's first complete serving publication (`afterInitialized`, the channel readiness waits for). No lifetime changes: UniFi assignments still expire 3 × Poll after their observation, truncated to the second; the operation, capture and request bounds, retry backoff and refusal backoff are unchanged. | UniFi assignments live three polls from their observation. Under the race detector on a loaded node the source acknowledgment needs two or three serving preparations (2 to 6 s), so observation to the next poll's last response took Poll + one sync + one acquisition, 13 to 19 s against 14 to 15 s, and a connected client lost its identity between two healthy polls (UNIFI-MARGIN baseline at 329f26f4: 4 of 20 pinned race runs failed the held capture, plus 2 readiness failures and 1 later convergence failure). Paced from the start the gap is max(Poll, one sync) + one acquisition. Parts (2) and (3) are what that change exposed: without them the poll that followed at once was fenced in 70 ms by the job's own status, or at commit by the target zone's committed-status write (resource version 65 against 2 in the cut, same generation), and a fenced observer waits a full interval. Part (4): every provider request commits a budget reservation that cancels the preparation in progress, so an acquisition at startup held readiness back for its whole duration (15 to 20 canceled preparations) and gained nothing. The ObservationScheduler contract already calls the interval a maximum polling interval. | Stopping per-request budget reservation commits from canceling serving preparations (the publisher and budget writers belong to another lane; D405 already coalesces Wakes, and the remaining cost is the acknowledgment's wait for a current publication, not the cancellations during an acquisition); lengthening the assignment lifetime or widening the test bound (changes a lifetime the observation contract pins, or hides the margin); a `time.Ticker` (a slow sync would wait for the next tick, up to two intervals); retrying a fenced observation at once (D284 keeps the schedule after a fence); exempting every status-only change of every captured object (only the target zone's own committed status is known to follow each commit) |
