# HA-OPEN7-FINISH decision rows

Both rows replace the rows of the same number in `out/HA-OPEN7/decision.md` and `out/HA-OPEN7/decision-D375.md` as a whole (which replaced HA-OPEN6's and HA-OPEN5's).

| D381 | RFC 2136 UPDATE from enrolled SQLite followers | An enrolled SQLite follower that is not the ready writer selects the zone in its own complete cut (NOTAUTH otherwise), uses the listener's verified TSIG proof and admits the key against its own current keyring (REFUSED for a missing proof or an unknown key). When its source changed under the request before anything was sent (it installed a newer commit, in a burst usually the previous update's own, or replaced a dependency, so the keyring is no longer current or the forward capture refuses the changed source), it waits, inside the forward's four-second bound, for its own current complete publication (`awaitFollowerPublication`), selects the zone and admits the key again there and captures the forward on that source. The same wait follows a source change that the forward finds only when it acquires the store for the exchange, before the exchange with the primary began (`EnrolledForwarder.Forward` joins `clusterreplica.ErrNotSent` to every error returned before the member client is called); the same command ID is then sent once. A command is sent at most once, and a refusal after the exchange may have begun is never followed by a second send. It then sends one version-1 `dns.update` member command: the exact request without its TSIG record, a `TSIGKey`/`tsig` subject naming the key and its secret-derived credential version, the algorithm, transport and signature deadline, bounded by four seconds, the DNS request and the signature. The primary runs the command on its current writer publication (`runMemberUpdate`): it waits for one within the command's bound (`Publisher.WaitTerm`) and captures it, admits the exact key identity (name, algorithm, credential version) in its own current keyring, then runs the native protocol adapter under its elected writer, so zone selection, grants, prerequisites, atomic commit and publication are identical. It runs the same command ID again, after the next publication, only when its SERVFAIL decided nothing that the command ID does not already retain (`memberUpdateRerun`): when `captureUpdate` rejected it only because its lease was no longer the current writer publication, its keyring stopped being current or the publication did not admit or route the zone at that moment (`forwardedUpdate.stale`, set before admission), when every compare-and-swap attempt of `Apply` lost against another writer of the zone and nothing committed (`pr.ErrConflict`, seen through `network.WithOutcome`), or when `Apply` decided it and only the zone's publication failed (`UpdateOutcome.PublishFailed`: the publication of its own commit, of a retained outcome the receipt ledger returned, or the recovery publication of an earlier commit; HA-OPEN7). Once an attempt committed, the next run finds its receipt and answers the retained NOERROR, and only that answer may follow (`memberUpdateRetained`); any other later answer (a key or grant changed since, a zone that began retiring) becomes SERVFAIL. A replayed command ID whose admission refused is final: 9397a519's `replay` condition (Deduplicated and not Published) also ran it again until the command's bound ended without an answer; the mounted test `TestRootMemberUpdateReplayRefusedByAdmissionIsAnsweredAtOnce` (a zone that stops allowing updates after the first commit) went unanswered after 20 seconds with that condition and is answered SERVFAIL in under a second with `PublishFailed` (HA-OPEN7-FINISH race03, race04), and the primary runtime's publication-failure test tells the two outcomes apart. A retiring or retired zone is refused at once, since no later publication admits it; a cause that does not clear ends at the command's bound with SERVFAIL. Every other answer is final. The command ID is the update's retry identity in the zone's receipt ledger throughout, so a repeated delivery or attempt returns the retained rcode without a second commit. The follower derives it from the exact signed request (`memberUpdateCommandID`: SHA-256 over the key name, algorithm and credential version, the transport and the whole message with its TSIG record, so message ID, time signed and MAC are included; HA-OPEN7): a client that got SERVFAIL, outcome unknown, and sends the same signed message again over the same transport, through this follower or another one, is answered from the retained outcome with the original NOERROR if the first one committed, and a message signed again, or sent over another transport, is a new update decided again. Only committed updates have receipts: the repeat of a refused one, or one whose prerequisite failed, applied nothing and is decided again on current data. So a once-sent forwarded UPDATE is answered SERVFAIL only when the forward did not finish within its four seconds (or the primary is unreachable, refuses the member, or the zone is retiring); the process tests send every forwarded UPDATE once, through the first follower and through the rejoined former primary, and require the primary's answer, and the slowest answer of each call in HA-OPEN7-FINISH was 0.2 to 1.9 seconds through the first follower and 0.7 to 3.4 seconds through the rejoined former primary (12 calls), against the four-second bound, so the margin there is thin on a loaded machine. Every forwarded UPDATE answered SERVFAIL is logged at warning level with its stage and cause on the follower and, when the primary decided it, on the primary. The zone runtime commits in its own transaction, fenced by the writer, the term and the admitted key identity; unlike a generic member command, the sender's current enrollment is rechecked in a transaction only when the acknowledgment is admitted, so a member revoked during those milliseconds can still commit and only its answer is withheld (such a member holds the TSIG secrets and could send a native UPDATE). The rcode is acknowledged inside the member request's command output scope (an empty member command after the zone runtime's own commit). The follower relays that rcode, signed with the request's key, under an acknowledgment bound to the verified key and unchanged empty response rather than its own data generation. A command that was not sent within the bound, was refused by member admission, or whose exchange was lost answers SERVFAIL and is never retried by either node; only the client's repeat of the same signed message reaches the same retry identity. Fenced, conflicted, standby and static nodes keep their local SERVFAIL. A native UPDATE sent to the primary is unchanged: right after a commit it can still be answered SERVFAIL before anything is applied. | The v0.2 UPDATE row requires primary routing once follower mode exists. Followers sit behind the same VIPs and DHCP configurations as the primary. DNS has no rcode for "outcome unknown", and RFC 2136 section 4.5 tells the requestor to try its next server on SERVFAIL, which is safe for an unsent update and is the native primary's answer for a committed but unpublished one. The receipt ledger already deduplicates by retry identity, and the zone runtime commits in its own fenced transaction, so the member command adds no second dedup row. Every commit is followed within milliseconds by its publication and by the node's own status and lifecycle writes (a new serial, a realigned lifecycle) and their publications, so a command that arrives right after one meets a publication that is being replaced on the follower, on the primary or both. | D375 | Multi-item requests through an enrolled follower | A follower bounds one 30-second lifetime, before body read, for every route whose effect it forwards as `objects.write`, `objects.delete` or `zones.command`: the `/objects` routes, `POST /apply`, the `/blocklists`, `/clientgroups` and `/policies` routes, `POST /{kind}:batchDelete` and the four zone convenience commands (25 registered routes, named by one predicate; a test observes which registered routes reach the forward hooks and holds the predicate to them). Every other `POST`, `PUT` and `DELETE` under `/objects/` and `/zones/` keeps the same bound on a follower, as it had before the predicate (`POST /zones/{zone}/notify` is the only registered one; it is not forwarded). `POST /apply` and `:batchDelete` send one member command for each document or name, in order, inside that single lifetime and keep their ordinary `200` answer with one result for each item. An item is reported with `unavailable` and its cause, is not applied and is never replayed when the lifetime has ended, when the request was canceled, or when the follower's own installed source changed since the request began. A forwarded request holds the follower's scheduled pull from before it captures its source until it ends (`holdMemberPulls`, `Coordinator.HoldPulls`, HA-OPEN7): a pull that is installing when the request arrives is waited for first (at most five seconds), so the request is bound to the installed source, and the scheduled pull then waits for every holder, at most `MaxPullHold` (30 seconds, counted from when the pull was due, so with overlapping requests it can come less than 30 seconds into the later one), and installs anyway after that. Explicit pulls (after a forwarded sign-in, sign-out or settings change) do not wait. The follower applies nothing itself and no item is retried or replayed; the hold only delays the next install. So a request is cut only by such an explicit pull, by the scheduled pull after 30 seconds, or by its own 30-second lifetime. The follower's own pull no longer cuts it; the lifetime does when the file does not fit. Each document is one exchange in which the primary commits before it answers, so the number of documents that fit is 30 seconds divided by the primary's commit time for one: a file of 200 DNS record sets took 11.9 seconds on the hosted runner (integrator, 329f26f4) and 17 to 26 seconds on the shared four-core machine, and with four test runs at once there the primary took 100 to 700 ms for each record set inside its command transaction (diagnostic e2e02: the follower's own work before the send was under 0.7 ms per item, the exchange was the rest; on the primary the wait before staging was 5 to 100 ms, staging and commit 200 to 500 ms) and the lifetime ended after 87 to 178 documents (HA-OPEN7-FINISH e2e02, e2e04). An apply of 200 documents through a follower is therefore applied completely unless a foreign commit intervenes or the primary needs more than 150 ms per document; the process test requires all 200 and fails on that machine under load, by the lifetime, never by the follower's pull. That last refusal of an item says what to do: "the follower's source changed while this request ran (...); this item was not sent and was not applied, and neither was any later one; send the items that were not applied, or the whole file, to the enrolled primary <name>, which applies them in one request" (`memberItemRefusal`, for contexts that `api.InObjectItems` marks). A request that began on a follower stays a forward after its context has ended: `ObjectForward` and `ZoneForward` read the mode of the request's original lease (`servinghttp.SourceMode`, which returns no token) and answer that refusal themselves instead of falling through to the node's local command. An item that was captured but not sent before the request's lifetime ended, or before it was canceled, names that cause ("the request's 30-second forwarding lifetime ended before this write was sent; it was not applied"), as a capture after the lifetime does; before HA-OPEN7-FINISH the ended context surfaced as "cluster authority changed" or "the enrolled primary is unavailable" (PUB-FOLLOWER review). An item whose exchange with the primary fails keeps the answer of a single forwarded write, `completion_unknown`; so does an item, or a single forwarded write, whose answer the primary sent but which the follower could not relay because its own source or membership changed during the exchange (before: `unavailable` "cluster authority changed", which said that nothing was made). In a multi-item request that answer adds that the later items were not applied and names the primary. Earlier items stay committed. There is no document cap and no new status. A capture refusal names its cause (source changed, lifetime ended, canceled, route not forwarded) instead of always reporting a changed source. | D362 already claimed `kudurru apply` and the dashboard editors, but only `/objects/` and `/zones/` paths were bounded, so capture refused every other route and reported a changed source. Apply and batch deletion already define per-item results on every node. The primary lets a request follow its own commits by an exact compare of command receipts (D378). Chaining a follower's later items in the same way was examined again (HA-OPEN5) and does not hold with the present protocol. On the primary the chain compares the provider coordinator's mark of the last state-changing command by identity, in process; a follower would need that mark as a serializable receipt in every member outcome, a new command field that the primary checks against its current mark before admission, and a rule under which the follower accepts a newer installed image, and a newer lease for its own pins and credential view, for a request that began on an older one, because its admission and the dependency pins of every forward are bound to the request's own publication (`CheckReadLease`). That is a member protocol change on both sides and a second admission rule for followers, not a fix, and this lane does not carry the D378 coordinator code of the release branch it would build on. So the original-source rule (capture once, never retarget) still forbids continuing a request across a source change on a follower. What changed the source was almost always the follower's own scheduled pull of the request's earlier items: HA-OPEN6 measured a 40-document file applied completely 5 of 18 times and a 160-document file never, and in one hosted run 84 of 200 new documents were refused. Holding that pull while the request runs keeps the source the request captured without retargeting anything, deciding anything on the follower or replaying an item (HA-OPEN7: the mounted test with 100 ms pulls was cut at document 4 without the hold and passed with it). The 30-second bound cannot be lifted safely in this release: a longer follower lifetime is cut by the scheduled pull unless the hold grows with it, and a hold of minutes stops replication, and the follower's view of a fence, for minutes; continuing on a newer installed source retargets the request (capture once, never retarget); forwarding the whole file as one member command does not help, because the primary still commits each document in turn and a member command is itself bounded to 30 seconds (`WithMemberCommandOutput`). On the primary the same file may take five minutes (`kudurru apply`), so a file that may not fit goes there. The earlier objection to deferring pulls (it holds replication, and fencing with it, and requests can follow each other) is answered by the bound: each scheduled pull waits at most 30 seconds, then runs while new holds wait for it, so replication runs at least once per interval plus 30 seconds even under a stream of requests, and fencing stays decided by the primary's term checks on every forwarded command (a follower that learns a new term later still has its forwards refused by a fenced old primary or by the new one). Reads on the follower can lag by up to that bound during forwarded writes. A partial result stays the honest outcome when the source changes anyway. Skipping documents that the follower's own view shows unchanged would answer a write from the follower's data, including when a document repeats a key that an earlier item of the same file changed on the primary, and committing no deduplication record for an unchanged result would remove the guard against a late replay of the same command; both were rejected. Such a file goes to the primary, which applies every document in one request.
